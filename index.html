What the real engine does, and what this does instead

Inside the Osmose

  • The sound engine is the EaganMatrix: a modular digital synth designed by Edmund Eagan, running on a DSP engine by Lippold Haken. It is the same engine as in the Haken Continuum and ContinuuMini.
  • In the Osmose it runs on six SHARC DSP chips and plays up to 24 notes, often as 12 stereo voices. There is no sample playback.
  • You patch it on a grid. Key gestures enter the matrix and pass through small letter-named formulas that scale, limit or combine them before they reach the synth blocks.
  • The blocks are five oscillators or waveshapers plus noise, five multimode filters, two filter banks of 8 or 48 filters, delay lines for chorus, flanging and physical modelling, shape generators, a convolution module, and a "recirculator" for delays and reverbs.
  • Haken's tutorials name the bank types: BiqBank, BiqGraph, BiqMouth, SineBank, Sine Spray, WaveBank, the Additive and Kinetic banks, and the Harmonic and Modal Manipulators.
  • Each key senses three things. Strike comes from the key's travel over time, which carries more than plain velocity. Press is extra travel past the normal key bottom. Pitch comes from rocking the key sideways. There is no front-to-back sensing.
  • The Osmose ships with more than 500 factory presets, each with six macros. You edit them in the Haken Editor, which runs in Cycling '74 Max.

What this page does

  • The EaganMatrix is closed. Its presets are patches for Haken's DSP, not code anyone can run, so this can't be a clone. It rebuilds the same synthesis families in an AudioWorklet and wires them to the same three gestures.
  • The 11 presets here are originals, one or more per engine. None of them are copies of Osmose factory sounds.
  • Every note gets its own strike, press, timbre and pitch, the way MPE works. An MPE controller plugged in over MIDI gets per-note bend and press too.
  • Laptop keys only know up or down. Strike comes from Shift or the Strike knob, and press comes from Space, the touch plate or a mouse drag.
  • Most laptop keyboards can only register 3 to 6 keys at once. Some chords will drop notes. That is the keyboard hardware, and the synth can't fix it. A MIDI keyboard doesn't have that limit.
